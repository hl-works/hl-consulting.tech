⚖️ OpenAI countersues Apple

OpenAI is now suing Apple in a California court, accusing the company of trade secret theft and of pressuring shared suppliers to shun its upcoming devices. It's a counterpunch to the lawsuit Apple filed this past summer. Just two years ago, the two companies were partners, integrating ChatGPT into the iPhone — today they're rivals, with OpenAI developing its own hardware. The story has been confirmed by multiple major outlets (FT, Bloomberg, Le Monde).

Dig deeper

In its countersuit, OpenAI accuses Apple of having "coerced and threatened" suppliers working with both companies, and of using employment clauses it deems unfair to prevent talent from leaving. Bloomberg notes that the trade secret dispute is "widening," with OpenAI accusing the iPhone maker of undermining its supplier relationships. Le Monde captures the dynamic: two former partners who, "month after month," are moving closer to full-on rivals, trading "blow for blow" in court. The backdrop: OpenAI is now building its own connected devices, putting it in direct competition with Apple on consumer hardware. For you, this is a sign of a realignment — the great AI alliances of yesterday are becoming the legal battles of tomorrow. Sources: Financial Times, Bloomberg, Le Monde, The Decoder

🚨 Grok (xAI) hit with US states' lawsuit

Several US attorneys general are suing SpaceX — parent company of X and xAI — to stop Grok from generating sexual images of children and non-consensual intimate content ("revenge porn"). The story got a lot of traction, and it echoes an investigation by Le Monde into so-called "abliterated" AI models — stripped of their safeguards and capable of answering nearly any dangerous prompt. The debate over model limits and moderation is back front and center.

Dig deeper

According to the attorneys general's statement (including Jay Jones and Dan Rayfield), the lawsuit targets the creation and promotion of child exploitation material and non-consensual images on the platform. At the same time, Le Monde published a deep dive into the world of "abliterated" (or "jailbroken") AI models: systems with their guardrails removed, accessible in just a few clicks, that answer the requests commercial competitors refuse — raising the question of who builds them and why. Meanwhile, the version of Grok capable of custom "bots" is generating serious buzz for professional use cases (more on that below), which illustrates the gap between product excitement and misuse risks. For you: if you're testing these tools, keep in mind that guardrail-free models exist and are circulating — and the legal framework is starting to tighten. Sources: Attorney General's office (via Hacker News), Le Monde

🧮 AI is shaking up mathematics, and the debate is heating up

Mathematician Terence Tao (Fields Medal) spoke at length on the Hard Fork podcast about how the latest models are transforming mathematical research — while flagging a key limitation: AI can solve a problem without being able to explain how it got there. On the other side, researcher François Chollet pushes back on the hype: in his view, our understanding of math has barely moved in 2026, far from the major breakthroughs of the previous 140 years.

Dig deeper

Tao, described as a "hero" by many AI researchers, spent decades working alongside tech companies; he now watches, like everyone else, as AI reshapes his field and the relationship between labs and academics. The Financial Times talks of surprising "computationally assisted discoveries" that raise questions about the future of human experts. François Chollet, meanwhile, calls out what he sees as "engagement farming": claiming that math has advanced more in 2026 than in 140 years is "ridiculously ignorant," he says. Commentator Ethan Mollick adds nuance: for now, you already need to be an expert in a subject to extract genuinely new discoveries from a model, because it's far too easy to generate "exciting" results that fall apart for lack of knowing which checks to run. Worth noting too: a Times article (flagged on Hacker News) reports that mathematicians fear AI could soon render online security "useless" (cryptography). For you: beyond math, this points to breakthroughs in abstract fields with very concrete downstream effects. Sources: Hard Fork / NYT, Financial Times, @fchollet, @emollick

🛍️ Black Friday: records at Shopify, AI agents crash the shopping party

Shopify merchants broke last year's sales record, hitting a peak of $6.8 million per minute, according to CEO Tobi Lütke and his team. The company's message: in the AI era, commerce is becoming "more human" again — and that's good news for entrepreneurs. Behind the scenes, a battle is brewing: Google, Amazon, Meta, and OpenAI are all vying to become the shopping assistant that helps you pick your holiday gifts.

Dig deeper

Shopify's leadership (Harley Finkelstein, Jessica Hertz) went all out on the celebrations: merchants featured on the Las Vegas Sphere, TV appearances, sellers hitting 10,000 and 100,000 orders right before Black Friday. Guillermo Rauch (Vercel) noted on his end that his infrastructure served brands like Porsche, Nintendo, Under Armour, and Crocs this weekend. Meanwhile, Bloomberg describes the rise of AI-powered "shopping bots" that threaten Amazon's and Google's dominance in product search — the real question is which assistant buyers will actually listen to. On the consumer side, several creators are already testing agents for personalized gift idea discovery. For merchants: the challenge in the coming months won't just be ranking on Google — it'll be getting in front of these shopping assistants. Sources: @harleyf, Bloomberg

🤖 AI agents enter the enterprise (Perplexity, Grok Bot)

Automation tools for professionals are moving fast. At Perplexity, "Teammates" — agents that execute tasks autonomously — saw their automations grow 27× in three weeks, with more automations launched than human messages sent during Thanksgiving, according to Aravind Srinivas. On the xAI side, custom "Grok Bots" are generating real buzz among investors and creators, who're calling it a "ChatGPT moment."

Dig deeper

Aravind Srinivas (Perplexity) describes a product that "works for you even when you're not working," positioning Perplexity as the workspace for small business owners to build their brand and marketing through rapid iteration — or even simulation. The Financial Times notes that AI agents are "arriving in large enterprises" and could boost competition, but that users remain cautious about handing over too much control. On Grok Bot, investor Gavin Baker (quoted by a16z) says he recreated — in 7 to 12 seconds, and "better" — tools that would have taken him hours to code: podcast summaries, newsletter digests, sentiment tracking on X and stocks. He calls it "another ChatGPT moment." Operators like Corey Ganim are already mapping out concrete use cases for running a business (multiple bots, weekly routines, connected apps). For you: these agents are targeting exactly the repetitive tasks that freelancers and SMBs deal with (monitoring, writing, follow-ups) — worth testing carefully. Sources: @AravSrinivas, Financial Times, a16z / Gavin Baker

🔬 Google DeepMind finds its second wind

Google is highlighting a new technique — DeepCrossAttention — that reportedly achieves comparable model quality up to 3× faster. It's a highly noticed publication on the technical side. Several observers are saying the lab is going through a genuine renaissance, unlike anything seen since Gemini 2.5, in a race where the number of "frontier" labs is nearly doubling every year.

Dig deeper

The Google research focuses on improving "residual connections" in transformers (the architecture at the heart of large models), enabling more efficient training. Analyst swyx sees it as a symptom of a broader dynamic: since 2023, the number of frontier or near-frontier labs has roughly doubled each year — what he calls "model neurodiversity" — a major macro factor that determines life or death for AI agent startups, and one that pushes back against the idea that raw compute alone drives the economics of the sector. For you, the main takeaway is that competition between providers (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Mistral…) is intensifying, which is pushing prices down and accelerating progress. Worth mentioning too: Mistral announces that five of its models rank in the global top 40 for audio transcription accuracy (its Voxtral tool), across multiple languages including French. Sources: Google Research (via Hacker News), @swyx, @MistralAI

✨ Quote of the day

"Getting humanity to change its habits fast enough is probably the biggest obstacle to AI's value." — a reformulation of an idea from Sridhar Ramaswamy: technology moves faster than our ability to use it.
